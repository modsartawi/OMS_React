071 — The derived dark twin

Prototype for ticket 071 of map 068. PosTheme.xaml is light-only — a till renders one theme — so every value approved in 070 needs a dark counterpart that does not exist upstream. The rule is the deliverable; the values follow from it. Nothing is applied — src/app/global.css is untouched.

The derivation rule

R1 — Dark keeps our proven lightness ladder and borrows only POS's temperature. Every neutral keeps the lightness of the dark theme we ship today (background oklch L .224, card .265, muted .307, sidebar .191 — levels already validated on real monitors across 40-odd screens) and takes its hue and chroma from the POS scale: H ≈ 250°, with chroma on the same C-vs-L curve PosTheme uses (C ≈ .005 near white → ≈ .026 near black). Warm near-blacks (H ≈ 84°) become cool near-blacks (H ≈ 250°). Nothing else moves. The dark app keeps a depth structure people already read correctly; only its temperature changes.
Today, in hex: page #1C1B19 · card #262523 · muted #302F2D · rail #151412. After R1: #121C27 · #1C2631 · #27313A · #0B151F. Same ladder, one hue rotation.
R2 — Chromatics lift to the L .66–.76 band, hold hue, and flip their ink. R1 cannot apply to --primary, the four severities or the two families: they are mid-tone fills (L .50–.62) designed to carry white text on white panels, and at that lightness they are near-invisible on a near-black card. Each keeps its POS hue exactly (255.9° / 152.8° / 72.0° / 22.4° / 161.9° / 285.0°) and its chroma within ±.02, and rises to L .66–.76 — the lowest band where --background works as ink at AA. Consequence, stated once: in dark, every filled chromatic control is a light tonal fill with dark ink. (White ink on the lifted fills measures 2.2–2.3:1 — see §4. There is no version of this where dark mode keeps white-on-colour.)
R3 — Interaction moves toward contrast with the page, not toward black. In light, --accent (hover) and --secondary-press sit below their base: darker = more present on a white ground. In dark they sit above it. This is the one place the ladder's rank deliberately inverts, and it is the same state-layer convention every dark UI uses.
R4 — -050 and -800 are roles, not lightness levels. -050 means "the quiet ground of this family", -800 means "ink on that ground". Their absolute lightness swaps in dark: -050 becomes a tinted near-black (L ≈ .32, C ≈ .05 — chroma roughly 4× the light tint, because a near-black needs far more chroma to read as tinted at all), -800 becomes a light tint (L ≈ .82–.87). Zero call-site churn: bg-danger-050 text-danger-800 keeps meaning exactly what it meant.
Why not reflect the light ladder about L = 0.5? It was the first candidate and it fails twice. (1) A literal reflection puts --card (L 1.000 → 0.000) below the page — a card cut into the page rather than raised off it, inverting the elevation language on every screen. (2) It compresses the seven surfaces into L 0.00–0.08, where our own shipped theme is evidence that steps that small disappear: today's dark uses ~.04 between tiers where light uses ~.015. R1 sidesteps both by not touching lightness at all.

Dark steps are ~1.7× the light steps and that is deliberate. oklch L is perceptually uniform in theory; in practice a near-black step is eaten by panel gamma, ambient reflection and OLED crush in a way a near-white step is not. Our shipped dark theme already encodes that correction (~.04 between tiers vs ~.015 in light) and R1 inherits it rather than re-deriving it. The ordering is what the ladder guarantees; the magnitude is empirical.

The family-fill question, decided

The ticket asks whether family buttons lighten or flip to tinted-outline on dark. Both are rendered; the proposal is tonal fill (left).

proposed — tonal fill + dark ink
Add noteReschedule Change StoreRequest Cancellation Cancel OrderForce Cancel
Weight order survives: the two terminal reds still outrank the family clusters, and the ghost still recedes.
rejected — tinted outline
Add noteReschedule Change StoreRequest Cancellation Cancel OrderForce Cancel
Every button is now an outline. 072's whole point — a quiet ghost tier below the coloured tiers, and a filled/outlined split inside danger — has nothing left to express.
Why fill wins. 072 spends the button's fill as its weight axis: ghost (add-note, return-document) < family fill < danger fill, with danger-outlined as the deliberate override step below danger-filled. Flipping families to outline on dark collapses four weight tiers into two and makes Force Cancel indistinguishable from Reschedule at a glance. The tonal fill costs one thing — the label ink stops being white — and R2 pays that price once, uniformly, for every chromatic fill.

Finding handed back to 070 / 077 — the light --attention fill fails AA. White on #B4791F is 3.69:1. It is latent today (072 gives attention no button; it appears only as a pill, where --attention-800 on --attention-050 measures 5.91:1 and is fine). But the moment anything fills a button with --attention, the light theme is non-compliant while dark is not. No ink rescues it: white 3.69, --foreground #19232E 4.31, --attention-800 1.82 — the hue simply sits at the luminance where nothing clears 4.5. So the ruling is a prohibition, not an ink swap: --attention is a pill / border / icon colour in light, never a filled-button ground. If a later ticket genuinely needs an attention-filled button, it needs a darker fill token of its own (#8E6318 carries white at 5.32:1) — it does not get to re-use --attention. Dark is unaffected: R2 lifts the fill to #E4A249, where the dark ink measures 7.83. Recorded here rather than silently patched, because it constrains an already-approved table.
Not in scope for this ticket. core/theme/ag-grid-theme.ts carries its own dark mode (9 params × 2 modes, hand-mirrored hex) — the --grid-* values in the mocks above are a suggestion to 074, which now has both halves of the palette to map from. The 249 raw palette occurrences include their own dark: twins (≈165 distinct decisions after pairing) and sweep in 077, which this table now unblocks. The two brand surfaces are 075 — note navy #002554 is darker than this dark theme's own page, which is new information for that ticket.
