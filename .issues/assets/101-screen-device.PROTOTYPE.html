After a run — capture 03-applied-and-potential: two lines, one fired bonus buy, one near-miss. Three frames: Items, Results, Promotions. The run strip is not a frame — it is a readout and a control cluster, so it gets no border and no heading. Net Total is weighted but not a hero: 099 ruled it the confirming number, and the promotion verdict — the thing the analyst actually came for — sits at eye level on the rail beside the lines it explains.

Before any run. The run strip is expanded as the form, because nothing has been set up yet and there is no run to condense; it collapses to chips on the first Process. Results and Promotions do not exist — no empty frame, no placeholder card, one sentence where the work area will be. That is the answer to "what does the screen cost when it has nothing to say": one frame. The three test levers (procedure key, loyalty group, loyalty tier) live here in the open form and never appear as chips unless set.

Promotion off — capture 01-plain-multiline: three lines, no promotion. 098 finding 3: turning promotion off blanks both applied and potential, so an empty rail here does not mean "nothing fired" — it means nothing was measured. The PROMO off chip is what stops the blackout being read as a result, which is exactly why 100 chips promotion in both states.

A warning line — capture 04b-no-price: COUP01 with no itemConditionControl. The line is the only coloured thing on the screen, its message sits inside its own expansion, and the fix — set Control to M — is one row up in the items frame. No count banner, no dot column: healthy lines are silent, so the eye lands on the one line that is not.

The whole run failed — capture 04a-unknown-material, HTTP 400 INVALID_UOM. 098 finding 5: this, not a per-line E, is how failure actually arrives. The banner replaces the work area rather than pushing it down, and the items frame stays exactly where it was so the offending line can be corrected in place.

What this prototype is asking you to rule

  • The headline. Three candidates on the switcher. A · quiet strip (drawn first, and the recommendation): the strip carries chips, money and controls, and scrolls away with everything else — because on 098's evidence the whole screen is 1–3 lines and ≤2 promo cards, so nothing scrolls and a pinned band buys nothing but a permanent 44px tax. B · sticky band: same content, pinned — worth it only if you expect baskets far larger than anything captured. C · totals row: the money leaves the strip entirely and becomes a right-aligned row under the results, making the results the headline and Net Total a figure among the lines. C is the most honest about "Net Total is a confirming readout" — and it costs you the ability to see the total while the run strip is in view.
  • The frame budget: three. Items (the instrument you retype every run), Results (the lines), Promotions (the verdict). Each buys a region with a different lifetime. Everything else — Summary, Actions, Manual Conditions, the detail panel, the Pricing Elements panel — is either dissolved into the strip or folded into a disclosure. Eleven frames become three, and one before the first Process.
  • Near-misses. Switch them off to see what the rail looks like as today's screen ships it. On 03-applied-and-potential the rail loses half its content and the analyst loses the answer to "what would ×2 have got me" — the exact question this loop exists to ask. Recommendation: keep them, as 099 ruled, and let this switcher be the evidence.
  • The pre-run state — is one sentence enough where the work area will be, or does the screen owe the analyst something more (a last-run recall, a worked example)?
