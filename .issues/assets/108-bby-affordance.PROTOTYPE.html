Form B carries a vocabulary conflict, drawn on purpose. Making the number the link means the number stops being a chip — 100 ruled no chip on this screen is ever clickable, and that rule is what makes "a chip is a readout" enforceable. B either breaks the rule or spends the number's chip to buy a link. Both costs are visible in the switch.

The near-miss card says a promotion exists and would have saved 26.04, and 098 found it carries no prerequisites and no item linkage, so the card cannot say what you would have had to buy. On a miss the details surface is the only place the rule is legible; on a fire it is a nice-to-have. Try Near-misses only.

The finding this ticket did not expect. bonusBuyInquiryApi.access() deliberately degrades a missing probe to granted — 404 / network → { screenAllowed: true, probed: false } — because for the inquiry screen, hiding a whole read-only screen behind an unbuilt probe is worse than showing it. Reused verbatim here, that rule produces the third tile above: today, on live SIS.Api, the button appears on every card and every click fails. The proposal drawn: in Simulation the affordance is gated on probed && screenAllowed — unprobed means absent — because a button that fails is worse than a button that was never there, which is the opposite trade to the one the inquiry screen faced. It is one boolean, and it makes the affordance ship dark until the endpoints land.

The mount cost — the third probe

099 recorded the consequence: this is a third access probe on mount, after Pricing/Access and the cache grant. Drawn as a sequence, the rule is that the rail never waits.

The probe is a screen-mount query, not a per-run one — it is fired once, shares the inquiry screen's cache key ['bonus-buy-inquiry','access'], and is almost always settled long before the first Process. The late-appearance case is therefore rare in practice; it is drawn because it must not be the case where the promotions are held back.
What this prototype does not touch. The detail surface itself (060's approved SAP mirror, 058's contract, 066's build), promoView's derivation, the endpoints, and the card's own field list (101 §6). The only new pixels proposed here are one control on a card.
