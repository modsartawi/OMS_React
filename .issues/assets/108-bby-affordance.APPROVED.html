At rest

One quiet control, last on the card

It sits below the amount, after every fact the card states — so the card still reads top-to-bottom as a verdict, and the way out is the last thing you meet rather than the first. It is a bordered control, not a chip: no chip on this screen is ever clickable, which is what keeps "a chip is a readout" true.

Why the near-miss carries it too. The fired card already explains itself — which rule, how much, which line. The near-miss card can say only that a promotion exists and would have saved 26.04: the payload carries no prerequisites and no item linkage for a miss, so the card cannot say what you would have had to buy. On a miss this button is the only route to the rule; on a fire it is a convenience.

The click

The bonus buy opens over the run, not instead of it

The screen's rule is that this run's own data expands in place and anything fetched fresh opens a modal. A bonus buy is a different record, from a different endpoint, under a different grant — so it is the one thing on this screen that earns a modal. The run stays behind it: closing returns you to the same result you were reading, with nothing re-run and nothing lost.

A grouping's members, opened

Both sides name what they match in one column, and the row says which kind it is. A material shows its number with the description beneath it. A grouping shows the grouping key and how many materials stand behind it — and that count is the way in. The list opens on top of the bonus buy, twenty at a time, because a grouping can stand for a thousand SKUs and none of them belong in the record above.

Unchanged, deliberately. This list is built and approved work from the Bonus Buy Inquiry effort. Nothing about it is restyled here — it will change appearance only when the app-wide move to this palette reaches it, which is a different effort's job and happens to every screen at once.

The grant

Three states, and only one of them draws anything

Permission granted
The control is present on every card. This is the picture above.
○2 PC for 29.95 SR
Would save26.04 SAR
Permission refused
The control is absent — no greyed-out button, no explanation, no reserved space. The card ends at its last fact and nothing on screen hints at a door you cannot open.
○2 PC for 29.95 SR
Would save26.04 SAR
Permission unknown
Also absent — and this is the change. The permission check answers by degrading to allowed when it cannot reach the server, which is right for a whole read-only screen and wrong for one button: it would put a control on every card that fails the moment it is pressed. Here, unknown means absent.
What we are choosing not to ship: “Couldn’t load the bonus buy.”

So the button ships dark. Until Bby/Detail is live on SIS.Api, the permission probe cannot answer and the control never appears — the rest of the rework lands without a dead affordance on it. When the endpoint arrives, the button appears on its own, with no further change to this screen.

The rail never waits for permission. The promotion cards render the moment the run returns. The check is asked once when the screen opens and shares its answer with the Bonus Buy Inquiry screen, so it is almost always settled first — but if it is not, the cards still arrive on time and the button appears a beat later. The promotions are never held back for it.

What it costs to build

One control, one shared module, one boolean

ChangeDetail
New One control on the promotion card, in both the fired and near-miss variants, plus its label.
Moved The detail modal becomes shared code rather than living inside the Bonus Buy Inquiry screen — it and its seven supporting modules move up to the shared layer, because two screens now open it and features are not allowed to reach into each other. Nothing about it changes; the Inquiry screen keeps using it exactly as before.
Changed One boolean: Simulation shows the control only when the permission check has actually answered yes, where the Inquiry screen shows itself when the check cannot answer at all.
Untouched The modal's design, the endpoints, the run's own data and every other region of the Simulation screen.
