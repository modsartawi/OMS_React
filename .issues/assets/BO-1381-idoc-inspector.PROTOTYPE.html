The shape of the IDoc inspector

One transaction's generated IDocs, without drowning. The hierarchy is transaction → N documents → N lines → N conditions, with payments, FI lines, item details, batch state and errors hanging off it. This device exists to settle which of those are screens, which are rows, and which are badges. Fake data shaped by BackOffice 1375; labels from 1376's dictionary. Throwaway — it does not become the feature.

OMS · SAP · IDoc inspector
One page, two levels of navigation, and that is the whole budget. A document is chosen in the rail (selection); a line opens in place (disclosure). Conditions and item details live inside the open line and are never a third navigation level — they are the reason you opened it. This is the simulation feature's grammar (SimResultsGrid + SimLineExpansion): plain fixed-layout table, twisty in the position cell, expansion in a colSpan cell so it can never out-grow its frame.
No AG Grid. The nearest solved problem in this workspace — lines with conditions — is where the simulation feature's last AG Grid was deliberately removed (SimLineExpansion's own comment: "seven rows do not need thirty rows' worth of grid chrome"). The inspector's worst realistic document is ~60 lines; its typical one is 4. A grid buys column resize and virtualisation nobody here needs, and costs the in-place expansion this shape is built on.
Two of this ticket's own questions were answered by 1375 before the prototype opened. ① There is no header-conditions pane, because there are no header conditions: IDocDocument.HeaderConditions is a Where("ItemNumber = 0") view and nothing on this rail ever writes such a row, so a pane would be permanently blank and read as a bug. ② The ConditionRate-not-ConditionValue rule belongs to the pricing engine's table, not to IDocCondition — 1375 says explicitly not to carry it into this display code. Both are drawn as absences here, which is the only way an absence gets reviewed.
Provenance is one column, not two. SourceTag is a chip on every line and every condition row — it is the answer to who minted this, and "" renders as a dimmed unknown, never as pos. ConditionSource is not a second column: 1376 established it is a near-constant M outside SourceTag = pos, so given its own column it would be a wall of M inviting the consultant to read keyed by hand. It rides as a small dotted-underline mark beside the tag, with the explanation in its title. The filter bar filters on the tag.
Download sits on the verdict strip, one button per IDoc type present — per BackOffice 1380: one transaction, one type, one file. Never per line, never per document (a per-document button would imply a per-document file, which is not what 1380 decided), and never a single button (AGG and FI are two files and pretending otherwise hides that they left in two batches).
